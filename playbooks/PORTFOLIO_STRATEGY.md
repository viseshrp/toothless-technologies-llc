# Account and product-portfolio strategy

The CEO maintains a balanced portfolio of target accounts, product categories,
engagement depths, and company capabilities. Opportunity selection is a company
allocation decision; product direction within an accepted engagement belongs to
the CPO.

## Opportunity scorecard

Score each candidate from 1 to 5 and retain the written rationale.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Public evidence | Is there a current, source-supported market signal? |
| Product significance | Is the user problem material and suitable for product intervention? |
| Strategic fit | Does the work fit Greyshore's digital-product mandate and current priorities? |
| Differentiation | Can Greyshore form a credible, distinct product point of view? |
| Commercial logic | Is there a plausible value and revenue case under stated assumptions? |
| Delivery fit | Can the required team deliver within free-tool and local-MVP constraints? |
| Risk | Are representation, data, regulatory, security, and reputation risks controlled? |
| Portfolio balance | Does the opportunity add useful account, product, or capability diversity? |

The CEO must explain the accepted tradeoff rather than selecting the highest
arithmetic score automatically.

## Portfolio archetypes

| Product context | Company capability deployed | Typical accountable functions |
|---|---|---|
| Public website or focused workflow | Conversion, information architecture, service design | Product, Design, Frontend, Product Marketing |
| Consumer mobile application | Retention, device behavior, trust, accessibility | Product, Research, Mobile, Analytics |
| B2B SaaS | Buyer-user distinction, roles, workflows, adoption | Product, Revenue, Design, Full-stack Engineering |
| Data-heavy internal tool | Operational outcomes, data quality, analytics | Product, Data, Backend, Operations |
| API or platform | Ecosystem value, governance, developer experience | Product, CTO, Architecture, Backend, Technical Writing |
| Existing product growth mandate | Funnel diagnosis, experimentation, positioning | Product, Analytics, Experimentation, Product Marketing |
| Regulated or trust-sensitive product | Privacy, security, accessibility, risk | Product, Legal, Security, Design |
| Multi-product portfolio | Capital allocation, sequencing, shared capabilities | CEO, CPO, CTO, CFO, Board |

## Concentration controls

The CEO and CFO monitor:

- repeated dependence on one product category or technology;
- excessive staffing demand on a single specialist role;
- target accounts concentrated in one regulated sector;
- portfolios dominated by build work without discovery or strategy evidence;
- portfolios dominated by strategy deliverables without working-product proof;
- repeated assumptions that remain untested across engagements; and
- company-level risks that require Board review.

## Board review

The Independent Board Director for Product and Strategy reviews the portfolio
when a proposed engagement creates material concentration, a major new product
or sector commitment, or an unresolved conflict between commercial, product,
and technology priorities. The director provides an advisory opinion; the Board
or CEO makes the decision within its authority.
