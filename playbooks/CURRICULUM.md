# Suggested CPO practice curriculum

The CEO should select fictional opportunities that build skills in sequence.
Real company names can change with current public evidence; the learning goals
should not.

| Case | Product context | Primary skill | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Focused website or simple workflow | Problem framing and MVP scope | A narrow user/problem choice, explicit non-goals, and a working core flow |
| 2 | Consumer mobile product | Discovery and usability | A research plan, prototype test, evidence-based iteration, and limitations |
| 3 | B2B SaaS | Stakeholders and prioritization | Buyer/user distinction, competing needs, roadmap tradeoffs, and acceptance criteria |
| 4 | Data-heavy internal tool | Metrics and SQL literacy | Metric tree, synthetic dataset, SQL-backed analysis, and a decision threshold |
| 5 | API or platform | Platform thinking | Ecosystem map, API consumer needs, governance choices, and sequencing |
| 6 | Existing product facing a growth problem | Experimentation | Hypothesis, causal risks, experiment design, guardrails, and interpretation plan |
| 7 | Regulated or trust-sensitive product | Risk and executive judgment | Privacy/security constraints, safer alternatives, and documented product tradeoffs |
| 8 | Multi-product portfolio | CPO leadership | Portfolio choices, investment allocation, product principles, executive narrative, and stop decisions |

Do not advance by calendar. Advance when the retrospective shows that the CPO
can perform the current skill with less coaching. Revisit earlier skills in more
complex settings rather than treating the sequence as a checklist.
